The interface, redesigned

Three artifacts and the document that explains them. The reasoning, the rejected alternatives and the token derivations are in docs/design-handoff.md, which is the thing to read first — these three are what it points at.

01 Firetower desktop — redesignedEvery screen and every state, as a specification you can click through. Fifteen tabs, and the one to open first is Every screen — a contact sheet of all 104 states at a third scale, which is what makes the other fourteen findable. The rest that are documents rather than mock-ups: Coverage (which feature is drawn and which is not), Spec (measurements, tokens, keyboard, the code delta) and Grouping (why six destinations).› 02 Firetower mobile — redesignedThe same recipe on a phone, with every pose at its real width — iPhone 390, Android 412, and the Duo’s three: 466 front panel, 669 inner portrait, 951 inner landscape. Carries its own Every screen contact sheet, and Coverage, Spec and Notes as the desktop one does.› 03 Firetower desktop — componentsForty components and six patterns, rendered live on the real stylesheet rather than described. Usage counts are measured by walking the desktop artifact — measure.py beside this page regenerates them — so “used on 50 screens” is a fact rather than a claim.›

Two caveats on the mobile artifact, which belong here rather than inside it. Everything in it was validated in an emulated viewport — no real device, no simulator, and none of the six gestures it specifies has been run under touch. And mobile/app.config.ts:26 is orientation: "portrait", so no window is ever 820 wide and the dock branch at app/workspace/[id].tsx:168 cannot currently execute — the 951 landscape pose the brief is mostly about is unreachable until that one line changes.

Unlike the other prototypes here, all three are self-contained single files — they carry their own tokens and their own fonts, because they are also the artifacts the design was reviewed from and had to survive being served on their own. They deliberately do not link desk.css. This landing page does, so it looks like its neighbours.

Every name, question and timestamp inside them is invented.
